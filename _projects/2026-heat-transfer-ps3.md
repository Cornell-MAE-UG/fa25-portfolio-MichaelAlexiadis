# MAE 3240 – Heat Transfer Problem Set 3

## Overview

This project highlights Problem Set #3 from MAE 3240: Heat Transfer. The assignment focused on several core heat transfer concepts including transient conduction, internal heat generation, thermal resistance networks, and composite wall analysis. I selected this assignment because it tied together mathematical derivations with physically meaningful engineering applications, especially in the turbine blade thermal barrier coating problem.

## Reflection

This homework assignment was one of the most valuable assignments for me in Heat Transfer because it forced me to move beyond simply applying equations and instead think carefully about the physical meaning behind heat flow and thermal energy balances. The first problem, which involved deriving the governing heat equation for a rod with convection and internal heat generation, helped me better understand where the transient heat conduction equation actually comes from. Working through the energy balance step-by-step made the derivation feel much more intuitive rather than something to memorize from lecture notes. I also became more comfortable identifying the assumptions required to simplify a physical system into a solvable engineering model.

The later problems were especially interesting because they connected directly to real engineering systems. The composite wall problem reinforced how temperature gradients behave differently in materials with and without heat generation, while the turbine blade thermal barrier coating problem showed how thermal resistance networks can be used to evaluate high-temperature aerospace hardware. As someone interested in spacecraft instrumentation and space systems engineering, I found the turbine blade analysis particularly useful because it demonstrated how material selection, coatings, and thermal management directly affect whether a component can survive in an extreme environment. This assignment improved both my technical problem-solving process and my ability to interpret what the results physically mean in the context of real engineering design.
