# Breathlete – Respiratory Performance Training Device

## Project Overview

Breathlete is an adjustable respiratory muscle training device designed to improve breathing strength and endurance for athletes. The project was completed as part of Cornell's Advanced Product Design course and followed the complete engineering product development process, from identifying customer needs through concept generation, engineering analysis, prototyping, intellectual property, and commercialization planning.

Our team developed a portable, app-connected device that allows users to adjust breathing resistance while tracking respiratory performance metrics over time. The design emphasizes usability, manufacturability, and performance while providing athletes with quantitative feedback during training.

## Reflection

This was one of the most comprehensive engineering projects I completed during my undergraduate education because it required integrating technical engineering with real-world product development. Rather than focusing solely on analysis, I learned how engineering decisions are influenced by customer needs, manufacturing constraints, intellectual property, cost, and market considerations. Developing the Product Design Specification taught me how to convert qualitative customer requirements into measurable engineering specifications, while preparing the provisional patent introduced me to protecting innovative designs and communicating technical concepts in a formal legal framework.

The project reinforced that successful engineering products require much more than sound technical analysis—they also require thoughtful systems thinking and careful trade-off decisions. These experiences are directly applicable to my interests in spacecraft hardware and scientific instrumentation, where performance, reliability, manufacturability, and documentation must all be considered simultaneously throughout the design process.
